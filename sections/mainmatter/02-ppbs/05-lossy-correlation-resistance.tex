In order to preserve soundness we will need a client to be unable to cause the PPB decryption process to terminate early.
This in turn means that they should be unable to craft lossy ciphertexts such that their decryptions appear to be valid ciphertexts for some non-lossy key. We refer to this property as lossy correlation resistance and define it formally here.

\begin{definition}[Lossy Correlation Resistance]
	Let $\pker$ and $\pkem$ be lossy public-key encryption schemes, with message spaces that are finite, cyclic, abelian groups.
	Let $g_M$ and $g_C$ be the generators for the message and ciphertext space of $\pker$, respectively.
	Let the variable $\badcorgame(\pker, \pkem, \adv, \secpar)$ where $\secpar \in \mathbb{N}$ denote the result of the following probabilistic experiment:

	\procedureblock{$\badcorgame(\pker, \pkem, \adv, \secpar)$}{
		\pkempp \leftarrow \pkem.\setup(\secparam) \\
		\pkerpp \leftarrow \pker.\setup(\secparam) \\
		\matpk_\ell := \pkem.\kgenlossy(\pkempp) \\
		(\matsk, \matpk) := \pkem.\kgen(\pkempp) \\
		(\revsk_0, \revpk_0) := \pker.\kgen(\pkerpp) \\
		(\revsk_1, \revpk_1) := \pker.\kgen(\pkerpp) \\
		(\mencr, \rencr, \otkgr_1, \otkgr_2, \otkgr_3, \otkgr^*) \leftarrow \adv(\pkempp, \pkerpp, \matpk_\ell, \matpk, \revpk_0, \revpk_1) \\
		\forall i \in [3]: \otpk_i := g_C^{\otpkgr_i} \\
		\pcreturn \pker.\dec(\revsk1, \\
		\t (\otpk_1^{-1} \cdot \pkem.\dec(\matsk, \pkem.\enc(\pkepp, \matpk_\ell, \otpk_3; \mencr)))^{-1} \\
		\t \cdot (\otpk_2 \cdot \pker.\enc(\revpk_0, g_M^{\otpkgr^*}; \rencr)) \\
		\t )
		) \neq \bot
	}

	The $\badcorgame(\pker, \pkem, \adv, \lambda)$ advantage of $\adv$ is defined as:
	$$
		\advantage{\badcorgame}{\pker, \pkem, \adv} = \prob{\badcorgame(\pker, \pkem, \adv, \secpar) \Rightarrow 1}
	$$

	We say that schemes $(\pker, \pkem)$ together satisfy lossy correlation resistance if for all NUPPT adversaries $\adv$, there exists a negligible function $\negl$ such that $\advantage{\badcorgame}{\pker, \pkem, \adv} \leq \negl$.
\end{definition}
