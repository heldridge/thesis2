We define and prove the security of our protocol in the global universal composability (GUC) framework~\autocite{TCC:CDPW07}, which extends the universal composability (UC) framework~\autocite{FOCS:Canetti01} to support global setup---allowing, for instance, modeling random oracles.
We use $\adv$ to denote the real world adversary, $\simulator$ for the ideal world adversary, and $\env$ for the environment.
We use $\randserver$ and $\aggserver$ to denote the Randomness Server and Aggregation Server, respectively, and $\user$ to denote a user.
We use $\func$ to denote (standard) ideal functionalities that are invoked by parties of the given protocol execution, and $\overline{\func}$ to denote global ideal functionalities which can be invoked by any party and even directly by the environment.
All functionalities are standard PID-wise corruption unless stated otherwise.
Our protocol is proved secure against a static, honest-but-curious adversary that corrupts either $\randserver$ or $\aggserver$, along with an arbitrary subset of users.
We refer the reader to \autocite{FOCS:Canetti01,TCC:CDPW07} for further details on UC and GUC.
