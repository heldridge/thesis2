\chap{Abstract}

Private protocols often require a \emph{threshold} at which privacy becomes compromised for the sake of utility.
For example, we would like our monetary transactions to remain private, unless they exceed a certain amount, in order to detect bad actors.
Similarly, we may wish for our internet searches to be hidden from an aggregator unless they hit a certain level of popularity, as learning the common searches across a population could be valuable.
While there is a large body of cryptographic work on threshold systems, many of the tools built remain unsuitable, often due to lack of efficiency, for use in the sorts of real-world protocols that need them.

In this work we propose cryptographic tools and protocols meant to solve real-world thresholding problems.
We begin by describing a scheme for allowing limited visibility into the transactions made using private cryptocurrencies, giving a regulator the ability to detect potential money-laundering while keeping the system perfectly private for honest users.
Next, we describe a new cryptographic primitive, \emph{multi-dealer secret sharing} (MDSS), that provides the ability to simultaneously reconstruct secrets shared by multiple dealers without knowing which shares correspond to each secret.
Finally, we show how MDSS can be applied by using it to build a protocol for private $t$-heavy-hitters that has stronger privacy guarantees than the state-of-the-art.
For both protocols we also give implementations and benchmarks, showing that they are efficient enough to be deployed today.
